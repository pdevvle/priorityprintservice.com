Why this shape

The current tool is nine accordion sections stacked on the left, a job strip, a viewport and a report that has to be unfolded. Every control is reachable, and every state is somewhere, but the operator has to assemble the picture: is this the approved file, is it vector, does the placed count match the priced count, what will the press actually receive. The draft turns that around. The truth bar says the state of the one thing that matters, all the time. The step rail is the setup order, and each step summarises itself while it is closed so the whole job is readable in one line. The inspector on the right answers "what is this file and what will print" without a scroll. The sheets stay in the centre at the same zoom they have today.

The truth bar

Four pills, left to right: the approval state against both hashes (verified P, raw R, unapproved, unbound); the colour verdict (vector or flat, CMYK or RGB count, intent); priced versus placed; the profile. Red pills are the ones you act on before sending. It replaces today's badges scattered across the toolbar, the Artwork section and the job strip.

Only the active step's controls are on screen. A step with a warning shows it in amber in its own line, so a problem is never buried in a closed section. The Specialty/Press-prep split of the current tool goes away: things that change page content (step 5 and Retouch) are separated from things that arrange sheets (steps 3, 4, 6).

The inspector

Three cards. This file: what was picked, its pages and size, vector or raster, colour, approval, the transforms the customer applied (from the manifest), and the other files in the folder with their roles, one click to swap. Decisions this run: the few switches that change content or add material, each with a one-line consequence. Before it leaves: the filename with its flags, where it files, and the full-run figures. Nothing here is new capability; it is the existing report, sorted by what the operator needs to know rather than by when the engine found it.

What stays

The engine, every setting, every flag token, saved setups, the queue (it becomes the landing view before a job is loaded, and step 2's picker once one is), the preview cap, the Missive path (same file, same tool). The controls keep the segment-not-dropdown rule. The sheets viewport and rulers are unchanged.

What it costs

A reshuffle of the controls column into a step model and a new inspector; roughly the size of the 1.60 reorganisation. The harnesses that drive segments by data attribute keep working if the attributes are kept. I would do it after Brief A ships and once the proofer's manifest carries transforms, since the inspector's best card depends on that.
